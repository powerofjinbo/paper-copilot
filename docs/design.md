# paper-copilot: a sentence-first, repo-grounded LaTeX writing platform (final design)

## 1. Product in one paragraph

paper-copilot is a local-first LaTeX editor (one command opens the browser) for a HEP researcher who writes English as a second language and wants AI help without AI flavor. Before any prose, the AI reads the analysis repository and the cited literature and builds a fact store and a draft outline that the author rewrites and approves. Writing then happens one sentence at a time, in two modes over the same outline and fact store: ghost mode (the AI proposes the next sentence in gray, grounded in facts and in the author's voice, and re-plans after every human sentence) and draft-then-review mode (the AI drafts a section; every sentence stays visibly "not yet yours" until the author accepts, edits, instructs or rejects it, with downstream sentences re-planned after each decision). The difference from Overleaf, Papers AI, Jenni and Prism: every AI sentence is governed by the author's own hep-paper-writing skill, conditioned on the author's own corpus and edit history, grounded (every number, cut and `\cite` key traces to an artifact locator or a resolved .bib key, or becomes a placeholder), logged per sentence so the venue disclosure writes itself, and never pushed to co-authors unreviewed. The sentence is the unit of thought, review, provenance and re-planning. First document: the near-final Canto manuscript.

## 2. Core loops

### (a) Ghost mode

Rule: the author owns the subject and the verb unless the section says otherwise. Per-section setting, visible in the Outline panel: `complete-my-sentence` (default for Introduction, Conclusions, Abstract: the AI continues only after the author has typed 3+ English words), `auto-propose` (default for Methods, Results, captions, where prose is formulaic and imitation works best; the AI may propose whole sentences at a boundary, the user's gray pre-writing picture), `questions-only` (no prose; the panel asks what the paragraph still owes). Ctrl+Space requests or regenerates everywhere.

1. Cursor enters an empty slot. No API call. The plan column shows the next move (`gap`, `result: f.g1.delta_points`); the Outline panel shows unsaid claims. What to say, not how.
2. Intent notes. A line that is mostly CJK is an intent line: teal tint, excluded from segmentation, lint and compile (compile runs on a shadow copy with intent lines blanked, line count preserved so SyncTeX stays valid), and blocks push. Ctrl+Enter renders it into one English sentence in the author's voice, grounded in facts (`ai-from-intent`); Alt+Enter renders 2-3 alternatives. The note moves to the sidecar and shows on hover.
3. Trigger: in `auto-propose`, a committed boundary (`.`/`?`/`!` not in the skill's ABBR set, next-char rule) plus 800 ms, or a 500 ms pause after 3+ words; in `complete-my-sentence`, pause only. Never per keystroke. Esc suppresses auto-ghost until the next boundary; two mismatching keystrokes dismiss; matching keystrokes keep it.
4. Request: stateless single shot, `claude-haiku-5-5`, `thinking: {type:'disabled'}`, `output_config: {effort:'low'}`, `max_tokens: 200`, schema with `text` first: `{text, fact_ids[], cite_keys[], fulfilled_move_ids[]}` (text first so truncation never loses the sentence). Cached blocks A, B, C (section 5); the volatile tail carries the last two paragraphs, the paragraph plan with `said`/`remaining`, the sentence prefix, the intent note, and six retrieved author sentences for this move. Constraints: continue the prefix verbatim, at most 35 words, no number outside the listed facts, `\cite` only from the allowed list, no new notation.
5. Gate (section 6, about 5 ms) runs on the whole sentence, so the ghost paints atomically; a dim pulsing dot in the plan column shows the request is live. One failure regenerates once with the failure as feedback; a second failure shows "no grounded suggestion".
6. Shown: one sentence, 45 percent gray, upright; fact-backed numbers dotted-underlined; placeholders as orange chips. The AI's further plan never enters the document; the next 2-3 moves appear faintly in the Outline panel.
7. Keys: Tab accept; Ctrl/Cmd+Right accept next word; Esc dismiss; Alt+] / Alt+[ cycle alternatives when they exist.
8. On accept the sentence becomes a unit on its own line (semantic line breaks), state `ai-drafted`, facts and cites linked, model and prompt hash logged.
9. Re-planning, three levels. Immediate, no API: fact ids and cite keys in the committed sentence mark claims `said`. By-product: every ghost response returns `fulfilled_move_ids[]`, so the plan advances without a second call. Asynchronous (`claude-sonnet-5-5`, `thinking: {type:'between_tools'}`, effort low): at paragraph end, or when `fulfilled_move_ids` is empty for two consecutive sentences (the author went off-plan), the re-planner receives the paragraph job, the sentences so far and the remaining plan and returns 1-4 revised intents with fact ids, or declares the paragraph complete and names the next opener. Visible effect: the move hint changes, Outline checkboxes tick, a stale ghost is withdrawn. The re-planner never edits text.

### (b) Draft-then-review mode

1. Entry per paragraph or section with an approved job and claim ids: `Draft from my notes` (default when intent notes exist) or `Draft from outline`.
2. Drafting: an Agent SDK session (`claude-opus-5-5`, skill plus the one reference the routing table names) with MCP tools `get_outline`, `get_plan`, `get_facts`, `get_sentences`, `get_lit`, `lookup_cite`, `propose_paragraph`. Per sentence: `{text, move, fact_ids, cite_keys, placeholders, note_zh, referee_question?}`. The PostToolUse hook runs the gate and returns failures as `updatedToolOutput`; after two retries a sentence lands as `unsupported` (red ring) rather than being dropped. Paragraphs land atomically, so a budget cutoff loses only the in-flight paragraph and `Continue drafting` resumes the session.
3. Structure pass first (the skill's top-down rule): drafted paragraphs appear as job cards to reorder, delete or rename before any sentence is shown; then `Start sentence review`.
4. Presentation: sentences enter the .tex one per line in state `proposed`: 60 percent ink, dotted gutter bar, a faint line above each paragraph stating its job. Nothing is black until the author acts. Alt+Down / Alt+Up jump between proposed sentences; the status bar reads `12 of 37 reviewed`.
5. Actions on the active sentence: Ctrl+Enter accepts; typing edits in place (`ai-edited`, gutter bar fills with edit distance); Ctrl+I instructs ("把基线放在前面", "verb first") and returns one rewrite as a word-level diff with the drift checklist (actor, scope, direction, condition) and a note in the user's language, frequent instructions becoming one-click chips; Ctrl+G grammar-only fix (minimal diff, state unchanged, `grammar_fixed` flag); Ctrl+. opens the sentence card, where single letters act: `l` alternatives (2-3 side by side, chosen with 1/2/3), `r` rewrite myself (clears the text, keeps intent and fact chips as a gray hint; result is `human` with `ever_ai: true`), `x` reject (the sentence becomes `[claim: <text>?]` so no claim is silently lost; the claim returns to `unsaid`; deleting the placeholder by hand is logged as "claim dropped by author"), `h` history.
6. Re-planning after an edit: 1.0 s after the author leaves an edited, instructed or rejected sentence, the re-planner reviews only downstream `proposed` sentences of the same paragraph and returns `keep | revise(text) | drop | insert_before(text)`. Revised sentences get a `↻` chip with an old-to-new diff; Tab on the chip jumps to the next. Accepted sentences are never changed automatically; the re-planner may only attach a comment ("now repeats the number in s14"). Section-wide re-plan runs only on the explicit button or when a claim is removed.

### (c) One outline, one fact store

Both modes read and write the same per-paragraph `Plan` `{job, claim_ids, said: {claim_id: s_id}, remaining: [{intent, fact_ids}]}`. Ghost pops `remaining` one sentence at a time; drafting fills it in one pass; both record `said`. Ghost and drafter prompts render the fact table from the same code path, so the two modes cannot disagree about a number. A paragraph may be drafted in B and the next written in A; states, gutter and card are identical.

## 3. Data model

Everything lives in `.paper/`, a sibling of the LaTeX tree, so nothing of the platform can reach Overleaf:

```
~/papers/canto/
  tex/                 Overleaf git clone (or overleaf-sync folder): the only thing synced
  .paper/
    project.json outline.json facts.json claims.json figures.json terminology.json
    evidence.jsonl history.jsonl        append-only; git-friendly
    paper.db                            SQLite: sentences, versions, lint, jobs (not versioned)
    brief/repo_brief.md brief/lit_brief.md
    lit/index.json lit/<texkey>.json lit/src/<arxiv-id>.tar.gz
    voice/corpus.jsonl voice/stats.json voice/memo.md voice/edit_pairs.jsonl
    sync/base/<file>.tex                last-synced snapshots (three-way base)
    build/                              latexmk output and shadow sources
    claude/.claude/skills/hep-paper-writing -> ../../../hep-writing-skill (symlink)
```

Sentence unit (ULID ids): `{id, file, start, end, line, hash, ctx_hash(prev+next), text_norm, section_id, paragraph_id, move, state, ever_ai, model, model_version, prompt_hash, created_at, accepted_at, edit_distance, intent_note, facts[], cites[], claims[], placeholders[], lint[], alternatives[], comments[], orphaned, split_from}`. States: `human`, `ai-from-intent`, `ai-drafted`, `ai-edited`, `proposed`, `external`, `placeholder`, `unsupported`; flags `grammar_fixed`, `ever_ai`. There is no "mark human-written" action: `ever_ai` can never be cleared, so disclosure counts use ever-AI-touched semantics even after an advisor rewrites the sentence. Normalization for `hash`: strip comments, collapse whitespace, math to `⟨M⟩`, `\cite{k}` to `⟨C:k⟩`, `\ref{l}` to `⟨R:l⟩`. Versions table `{s_id, version, ts, text, origin, model, prompt_hash, instruction, lang, parent_version}`, origin in `{human_typed, human_edit, ai_ghost, ai_intent, ai_draft, ai_rewrite, grammar_fix, replan, external_edit}`; `history.jsonl` mirrors it.

Outline (`outline.json`): `{story{text, key_phrase}, gap, enabler, ladder[{case, fact_ids}], claim_figures[{claim_id, figure_id}], headline{fact_id, baseline_fact_id, condition}, framings[], sections[{id, title, question, template, ghost_mode, paragraphs[{pid, job, moves[], claim_ids, intent_notes, plan}]}]}`; every item carries `provenance: author | ai-proposed | ai-edited`.

Fact (HEP-Pilot results-manifest shape, extended; worked example from the real Canto file):

```json
{"id":"f.g1.delta_points","kind":"number","value":2.319,"sigfigs":3,"unit":"points",
 "uncertainties":{"total":0.279,"boot":0.164,"seeds":0.226},
 "definition":"Efficiency difference, Canto-L (ttbar) minus acorn (HNL)+LM, population P_G, generic_iso union test, n=8710",
 "definition_source":"derived from claim.A, claim.B, population, split, n_targets",
 "artifact":"results/addendum_g_g1_v1_claim.json","locator":"#/delta_points",
 "artifact_sha256":"...","fingerprint":"sha256(sha||locator)","stale":false,
 "validation_state":"candidate","aliases":["2.3 points","2.32 points"]}
```

`kind` in `{number, cut, definition, sample, setting, figure, table}`. Aliases are derived from value and sigfigs, never free text. `validation_state` defaults to `candidate`; `reproduced` or `independently_validated` only through an `evidence.jsonl` record `{fact_ids[], command, artifact, sha256, ts}` (HEP-Pilot's `independent_validations` idea). `outcome: holds` in a claim file is a claim outcome, not a reproduction. Claim map (note-writer contract): `{claim_id, text, evidence[fact_id], citations[texkey], qualification, status: supported|placeholder|excluded, sentence_ids[], section, move}`. Figures: `{figure_id, path, sha256, producer_script, claim_ids, caption_s_ids, exists|needs_run}`. Voice: `corpus.jsonl` rows `{text, source, section_type, unassisted}`, `stats.json` (author norms beside the skill's corpus norms), `edit_pairs.jsonl` `{s_id, ai_text, human_text, instruction, section_type, grammar_only}`, `memo.md`. Lint: `{s_id, check, source: style_check|kobak|heuristic, msg, word, corpus_rate, user_rate, dismissed}`.

Anchor survival. Live: ranges map through every CodeMirror transaction (`ChangeSet.mapPos`); only the edited paragraph is re-segmented. External change (pull, overleaf-sync, file changed on disk): three-way with `sync/base`. Exact `hash` match re-anchors (with `ctx_hash` as tie-breaker for repeated sentences); else the best candidate within 8 lines with normalized similarity >= 0.6 and an agreeing neighbor re-anchors as `external` with an `external_edit` version; a one-to-two split keeps the id on the best match and links the other via `split_from`; otherwise the unit is `orphaned` and listed for reattach or discard (metadata is never deleted automatically); new lines become `external` sentences. Import offers a reversible one-sentence-per-line normalization of prose paragraphs, with the co-authors' consent, because it makes git merges and alignment sentence-granular.

## 4. Grounding pipeline

All agents run read-only: `tools: ['Read','Glob','Grep','Skill']`, `disallowedTools: ['Bash','Write','Edit','WebFetch','WebSearch']`, `permissionMode: 'dontAsk'`, a PreToolUse hook that denies any Write/Edit/Bash absolutely, `maxBudgetUsd` per run, `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_AUTO_MEMORY=1`.

- Deterministic extractor (Node, no model): registers every number in `results/*.json`, `*_claim.json` and CSV summary rows as a fact with artifact path, JSON pointer or row/column locator, SHA-256 and fingerprint; definitions come first from artifact metadata columns (`sample, population, model, label, n, split`) so a value cannot be mislabeled; `verify_fact` re-reads the artifact at the locator on every open and marks `stale` when the fingerprint changes, queuing every linked sentence for the Consistency view. On Canto this yields `eff_A`, `eff_B`, `delta_points`, `sigma_points`, the CSV efficiency table, and the cuts in `docs/definitions.md` (pT > 1 GeV, |eta| < 4, >= 5 space points).
- `repo-reader` (`claude-sonnet-5-5`, between_tools, medium effort, `cwd` sidecar, `additionalDirectories: [repoDir]`): reads README, docs/, results/, configs/, figure producers and decision logs and returns `repo_brief.md` (<= 6k tokens: project summary, pipeline stages in the repo's own names, samples and definitions, result tables with the artifact behind each cell, claims register from `docs/preregistration.md`, figure inventory with `exists|needs_run`, terminology, unknowns) plus fact candidates as locators only (`{proposed_id, definition_paraphrase, artifact, locator, kind, condition}`); the backend reads the values. Numbers that exist only in README prose become `candidate` facts flagged "prose-only".
- `lit-reader` (Sonnet) reaches the outside world only through in-process MCP tools, so it cannot invent records: `inspire_lookup` (batched OR queries, `format=bibtex`, token bucket 15 per 5 s, cached), `arxiv_src` (tar.gz, bare gzipped .tex, PDF-only; one request per 3 s), `s2_lookup` (1 per s, key). It resolves every .bib entry (@retorquere/bibtex-parser) to an INSPIRE texkey and writes `lit/<texkey>.json` `{texkey, recid, arxiv, title, abstract, quotable:[{claim, quote, locator}], usable_for[]}`, the shape of the user's `docs/paper_facts.md`, for the 5-10 closest papers.
- Citation validation: a key must exist in the .bib and be `resolved` on INSPIRE or Semantic Scholar, or `user_confirmed`; prompts receive only such keys with one-line descriptions; the gate turns any other key into `[ref]`.
- `outliner` (`claude-opus-5-5`, skill plus story-and-structure.md) runs only after the author fills the five-item form, and proposes three framings, entered as `ai-proposed` items.

Routes: `POST /agents/read-repo`, `POST /facts/rescan`, `PATCH /facts/:id` (confirm, rename, exclude), `POST /lit/resolve`, `GET /lit/:key`, `GET|PUT /outline`.

## 5. Skill and voice system

Loading: the skill stays the user's git checkout, symlinked at `.paper/claude/.claude/skills/hep-paper-writing`; agents run with `cwd: .paper/claude`, `settingSources: ['project']` (an empty list loads no skills), `skills: ['hep-paper-writing']`, `Skill` in `tools`; the analysis repo and `tex/` come in through `additionalDirectories`. Progressive disclosure: SKILL.md always; one or two references by the skill's routing table (outline, abstract, intro, conclusions: story-and-structure; section drafting: layering-and-flow; ghost and rewrites: sentences-and-words; captions and results: figures-and-evidence), each wrapped in `<reference fictional_examples="true">`; `style_check.py` runs in the sidecar and never enters context. The direct-API path reads the same SKILL.md from disk into block A.

Prompt layout, identical for Haiku, Sonnet and Opus (caches are per model): block A `[SKILL.md + reference + memo.md + fictional-examples rule]`, `cache_control: {type:'ephemeral', ttl:'1h'}`, about 12k tokens; block B `[repo brief + literature brief + frozen outline (five items and section jobs) + terminology + allowed cite keys + compact fact table]`, 5-minute TTL, 15-20k tokens, refreshed by use and by a `max_tokens: 0` keep-alive every 4.5 minutes while the window is focused; block C `[exemplars for this section type]`, 5-minute TTL; volatile tail: the live paragraph plan, context, prefix, intent, retrieved move sentences. Model, thinking and effort are constants per route. Pre-warm A+B on open. Whether a `max_tokens: 0` pre-warm may carry `output_config.format` is unverified; Phase 0 settles it with two calls (pre-warm with and without `format`, then a real request, reading `cache_read_input_tokens`); if it cannot, ghost falls back to plain text with a stop sequence and a trailing `facts:` line, parsed by the gate.

Voice, four sources at once because few-shot imitation alone is weak: (1) the author's unassisted corpus (the FCC-ee and LDMX notes, the thesis chapters the author confirms; not the TIME paper, whose text the author did not write; the Canto README is not assumed unassisted); (2) measured norms of that corpus beside the skill's norms as numeric targets; (3) the six nearest author sentences for the current move and section type (BM25 over normalized text); (4) a CIPHER-style memo (<= 400 words of plain preferences with counts and pair ids) refreshed by Sonnet at idle time when 10 new edit pairs with edit distance > 10 percent accumulate; grammar-only pairs are excluded so the memo never learns the model's register; the memo is visible and editable, and deleting a bullet is a legitimate correction. Anti-anchoring: the author writes the five items before the AI proposes; three framings, not one; the AI never writes the first words in voice-critical sections unless asked; alternatives on demand only; Chinese intent rendered into the author's English and kept beside the sentence; `questions-only` available everywhere; outline items marked `ai-proposed` until edited.

AI-ese linter: the Kobak excess-vocabulary list flags words with excess ratio >= 3 or in the top 100 and shows the ratio; pattern phrases ("not only ... but also", "serves as", "plays a crucial role") are labeled heuristics with no rate. Style-distance meter: two needles (to "you", to "corpus") per paragraph, section and document from a fixed feature set (median and p90 length, verb position, passive share, we-rate, hedges and intensifiers per 1k, connector openers, inflated and excess vocabulary, type-token ratio, length variance, dash rate); clicking lists the three sentences pulling the draft furthest from the author; no per-sentence AI score, no detector as a gate. Language: paper text in English; notes, questions and replies follow `notes_language` (default zh when the author types Chinese); no CJK ever reaches a push.

## 6. Faithfulness and provenance

One pure function `gateSentence(text, ctx)` runs in the ghost path before display, in the PostToolUse hook, and on every rewrite:

1. Numbers: every numeral with unit, percent, +-, exponent or `\SI{}` form must equal a fact at its sigfigs or an allowed rendering (alias), or be a section, equation, figure or year token, or be a declared derived relation (`2.4 times` from two facts via a small calculator), or be a number typed in the intent note (linked as `unlinked`, amber, visible to the Consistency view); otherwise `[fact?: <description>]`. Percent-versus-fraction and factor-versus-percent mismatches are flagged.
2. Cuts and definitions match facts of kind `cut`/`definition` or become placeholders.
3. Citations outside the resolved set become `[ref]`; `\ref`/`\eqref` targets must exist in the flattened project.
4. LaTeX invariance for rewrites: identical multisets of `\cite`, `\ref`, `\label`, `\eqref` arguments, math spans and macros, else rejected ("the AI changed notation").
5. No number from the precomputed set of the skill's fictional-example numbers.
6. Placeholders descriptive and unique; export and push blocked while any remain.
Rules added after the adversarial review (each backed by a regression test): quantities written in words ("ninety-seven percent", "twice", "an order of magnitude") are read as numbers (`loose_number`); small integers are checked when they carry a unit, sit in a comparison or count a result; an uncertainty must belong to a fact linked in the same sentence; a rewrite must keep every number of the original (`number_changed`) and its direction words and negations (`direction`, `meaning_changed`); a "grammar-only" fix that changes the meaning is offered as an AI rewrite, never applied under the sentence's old state; unescaped `%` and unbalanced math are `bad_latex`; any internal error fails closed (`gate_error`).

7. Claim strength, deterministic lexicon: "exclude" or "rule out" needs a limit fact with CL >= 95 percent; "demonstrate", "establish" need `validation_state` >= `reproduced`; a result verb (improve, better, higher) with no claim id is flagged (echoing the vague-claim check). Reread hints, never errors.
8. Tier-1 style_check and AI-ese.

Sentences are never silently repaired. Provenance: every version row is an event in `history.jsonl` with model id from the API response, prompt hash, instruction, language, before and after hashes and edit distance. The disclosure generator (`GET /disclosure?venue=aps|elsevier|arxiv|springer`) reads the log and emits an editable `disclosure.tex` in the venue's position: APS (tools and versions; how they assisted, with counts: N sentences drafted from the authors' outline and result files, M edited by the authors with mean edit distance X percent, K rendered from the authors' notes, L language-polished; how verified: every number and citation checked against result files and bibliography, every sentence reread by the authors; records kept at `.paper/history.jsonl`), Elsevier's titled declaration above the references, a JHEP/Springer Methods paragraph, an arXiv comment line, plus `ai_use_record.json` to keep.

## 7. UI layout

Layout (since the author's first use on a Mac, Nov 2026): a narrow **left rail of four steps in writing order**, one resizable step panel, the editor in the center, the PDF on the right, a top bar and a status bar.

- **① Plan**: the five Step-0 items (author first; drafting stays locked until they are written and attested), alternative framings, the section and paragraph plan with job cards, Draft buttons, per-section ghost mode.
- **② Sources**: two sub-tabs. *Facts*: searchable table (id, rendered value, definition, state, stale badge, artifact and SHA), where-used, manual entry (always `candidate`), and the unresolved-numeral list of the whole `\input` tree with file and line. *Literature*: cite keys, INSPIRE resolution, confirmation.
- **③ To do**: one merged list in document order of proposed and unsupported sentences, placeholders and referee questions (at most four per section, in the notes language, never inserted), with progress.
- **④ Check & submit**: the submission check (blockers, warnings, info; each entry jumps to its sentence, also across files), Consistency, Terminology, Figure plan, the disclosure text, and Sync with Overleaf (pull, push check, push).
- **Top bar**: breadcrumb with the file switcher (multi-file projects), the compile pill (Compile, Show/Hide PDF, error count; *No LaTeX* with install lines for the platform when `latexmk` is missing; *Example PDF* in the browser demo), Sync menu, Check pill (last submission verdict, refreshed after edits and compiles), the "Simulated AI" badge without an API key, ⚙ and **?** (shortcuts, guided tour, reset the demo).
- **Settings drawer (⚙)**: voice (memo and style meter), interface language (中文 / English), theme (system, light, dark), ownership heatmap, PDF beside the editor, session API cost.
- **Auto-fit**: the editor keeps at least about 560 px. When it would get narrower, the PDF yields first, then the step panel; a yielded pane leaves a 30 px strip that shows it is there (for the PDF, the compile state) and brings it back on click. A pane the author opened explicitly is pinned; when both are pinned and do not fit, the one pinned last wins. A compile the author started pins the PDF.
- **Onboarding**: a first-run hint for the four steps in the full app; in the browser demo (`?mock=1`) an 8-step guided tour on the example paper (spotlight on the real element, one task per step, 中文/EN, platform keys), and Compile shows a committed example PDF with approximate SyncTeX. The tour never runs on the author's own paper.

The older panel names (Outline, Facts, Review, Placeholders, Questions, Consistency, Terminology, Figures, Disclosure, Sync) still work as deep links; each maps to its step and section (apps/web/src/components/steps.ts). Voice lives in Settings.

Editor: CodeMirror 6 with `@codemirror/legacy-modes` stex (MIT; codemirror-lang-latex is AGPL), soft wrap, one sentence per line, sentences as mark decorations, the sentence under the cursor at 4 percent tint. Gutter, three narrow columns: (1) provenance bar: none `human`; dotted gray `proposed`; blue-gray `ai-drafted`; blue-gray with fill proportional to edit distance `ai-edited`; teal `ai-from-intent`; amber `external`; red ring `unsupported`; orange dot placeholders; (2) lint dot with count on hover; (3) plan column: move label on the active paragraph, `↻` for re-planned sentences, pulsing dot during a ghost request. Sentence appearance: `human` and accepted AI states in full ink (state lives in the gutter, not the page); `proposed` 60 percent ink; ghost 45 percent gray; intent lines teal; placeholders orange chips. An "ownership heatmap" toggle tints backgrounds by state for a pre-submission scan. Sentence card (hover 400 ms or Ctrl+.): state, model, time; move and paragraph job; fact chips (`f.g1.delta_points = 2.3 +- 0.3 points`, click opens the Facts entry with artifact and SHA); cite keys with titles; the Chinese note; lint flags with rule id, corpus rate and dismiss; actions.

Panel contents. Outline: five editable items with the key phrase highlighted wherever it occurs; section tree with paragraph jobs, claim chips (unsaid, said with jump, dropped), drag to reorder, per-section ghost mode, Draft buttons. Facts: searchable table (id, rendered value, definition, state, stale badge, artifact), where-used, unresolved `[fact?]` list, manual entry (always `candidate`). Review queue: proposed sentences in order with progress. Questions: at most four referee questions per section, anchored to sentences, in the notes language, never inserted. Consistency: each headline number across abstract, body, conclusions and captions with drift, factor-versus-percent and dropped-condition flags, plus stale-fact sentences. Terminology: term, definition, first use, variants; feeds `style_check --known`. Figure plan: job, claim, caption-anatomy checklist, citation order, exists or needs run.

PDF: PDF.js; compile on save (2 s debounce) via `latexmk -pdf -synctex=1 -interaction=nonstopmode -outdir=.paper/build` on the shadow copy; Ctrl+Shift+J highlights the current line's box (`synctex view`); a PDF click selects the source line, which is one sentence; a log drawer links errors to lines. Overleaf: Phase 1 ships `paper pull` and `paper push` commands; Phase 2 the Sync panel. Pull runs `git pull --rebase` (or `ols`), re-anchors and lists "co-authors changed 14 sentences" as `external` with diffs. Push shows the exact diff and is refused while `proposed` or `unsupported` sentences, placeholders or intent lines exist (proposed sentences can be parked in the sidecar); never force, never rename from the platform.

### Features added in Phase 2 and the second review (Nov 2026)

- **Submission check** (`packages/core/src/meta/submission.ts`, `GET /api/report/submission`): one pure checklist. Blockers: unreviewed and unsupported AI sentences, placeholders, intent lines, CJK outside comments, merge conflicts, compile errors (or no compile of the current text), undefined cite keys, AI numbers that match no fact or state one the wrong way round or for the other method, stale facts still stated, a missing disclosure statement. Warnings and info below. It reads every typeset file of the `\input`/`\import` tree.
- **Consistency (echo check)**, **Terminology**, **Figure plan**: the Phase 2 panels, now sections of ④.
- **Derived numbers** (`packages/core/src/meta/derived.ts`, `gate/sides.ts`): a ratio, relative change, difference or share of two facts grounds a number only in the form of its kind (a relative change only as a comparison, never as an absolute value; no efficiency above 100 percent), only when the sentence names both sides or the quantity with a reference word, and only in the direction of the sign (comparative word and subject order). A factor ("3 times", "twice") matches only ratio facts, never a count or a points value, and a word or whole-number factor must be within 10 percent of the ratio. "N out of M" needs a round M or a count fact.
- **Attribution**: the A=/B= sides in a fact's metadata are matched against the method named next to the numeral; a value given to the other method blocks when the binding is explicit ("96.94% for acorn") and warns otherwise.
- **Review state survives outside edits**: an edit made outside the app (editor, `git pull`, Overleaf) to a `proposed` or `unsupported` sentence keeps that state and adds an `external_edit` version, so it still blocks push and the submission check.
- **Stale facts stay blockers**: re-linking never drops a link to a stale fact while the sentence still states the old value (restart, unrelated edits, ambiguous numerals, renamed fields).
- **Repo watcher**: the fact files of the analysis repo (`results/**/*.json`, `*.csv`, `docs/definitions.md` by default) are watched read-only; a change triggers one debounced rescan that marks changed facts stale and a notice naming the changed files.
- **Restore**: the sentence card's history puts an earlier version back as a new version with that version's provenance; an AI version is re-checked by the gate against the current facts (a number that no longer holds makes it `unsupported`), a restored AI draft needs review again, and `everAi` is never cleared.
- **Multi-file**: the file switcher lists `main.tex` and its inputs with sentence counts; cross-file reveal from any panel row; checks cover the whole tree.
- **Data safety**: the editor never overwrites an edit made on disk; edits far apart merge exactly; text typed while the server is down is kept and sent after reconnect; a tab with unsent text is never discarded silently.

## 8. Architecture

Processes: (1) browser front end (Vite, React, CodeMirror 6, PDF.js); (2) Node backend (TypeScript, Fastify, one WebSocket): project store and SQLite; segmenter (unified-latex positions, mask math/`\cite`/`\ref`/`\label`, split with the skill's ABBR list and next-char rule, slice the original source by offsets); anchor and three-way service; ghost service (direct Messages API); agent service (Agent SDK); literature client with rate limiters and cache; compile service (latexmk, synctex, file watcher); sync service (git, overleaf-sync subprocess); lint orchestrator; disclosure generator; a `client-core` package holding the ghost state machine and segmenter contracts for later clients; (3) Python sidecar `lintd.py`: long-lived, JSON lines over stdio, imports `style_check` once, wraps `style_check.flag` at runtime to attach `Sent.i`, `block` and `raw` without touching the user's file, serves `lint.sentence {text, part, known[]}` (sentence-local checks), `lint.document {flattened_tex}` (all checks, reconciled to units by line and normalized excerpt), `corpus.stats {texts[]}`; (4) latexmk and synctex.

APIs: WebSocket `doc.changes` (CodeMirror change sets; the backend keeps a mirror), `sentences.updated`, `ghost.request|result|cancel`, `sentence.action`, `plan.updated`, `lint.update`, `compile.status`, `sync.status`, `job.progress`; REST `POST /ghost`, `POST /sentence/:id/{instruct|alternatives|grammar|accept|reject}`, `GET /sentence/:id/history`, `POST /agents/{read-repo|draft|review}` (SSE jobs), `GET /synctex/{forward|inverse}`, `POST /sync/{pull|push}`, `GET /style/distance`, plus the grounding and disclosure routes above.

## 10. Risks and open questions

1. Ghost latency above 1.2 s feels broken. Mitigation: Phase 0 measures; on-demand default outside `auto-propose` sections; atomic paint; Sonnet promotable by the blind test.
2. Haiku at low effort may leak reasoning text or write flat English. Mitigation: structured output or stop sequence, the gate, one regeneration, the blind test.
3. Chinese IME with a widget at the cursor. Mitigation: suppress ghost between `compositionstart` and `compositionend`; block widget below the line as fallback; test both browsers in Phase 0.
4. Co-author edits break anchors or reflow lines. Mitigation: semantic line breaks, fuzzy alignment, orphan list, no automatic metadata deletion. Question: who owns the Canto Overleaf project and on which plan (git bridge or overleaf-sync); will Frankenthal accept one-sentence-per-line .tex?
5. Facts stale or ambiguous (README versus `results/`). Mitigation: locator-only extraction, fingerprint staleness, default `candidate`, prose-only numbers flagged. Question: which files are authoritative in each repo?
6. Voice corpus small or already AI-touched. Mitigation: ask which texts were unassisted; lean on measured norms and the memo; show the meter with corpus size. Question: which of the FCC-ee, LDMX and thesis texts were written without AI?
7. The outline anchors thinking. Mitigation: author-first form, three framings, outline provenance, `questions-only` mode.
8. Scope, data and licensing: one developer; unpublished results leave the laptop through the API; AGPL contamination. Mitigation: frozen stubs and weekly acceptance; local-first, key in the keychain, `.paperignore`, Anthropic's data policy documented; MIT/Apache dependencies only. Question: confirm the local-first web app as first client and Canto as the Phase 1 document.
